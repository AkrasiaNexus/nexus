# Idiolect context rules

Words that are also real English words. "its" can be the possessive or a missing-apostrophe "it's"; "ill" can mean sick or "i'll". These rules change them only when the neighbouring words make it clear.

The word lists come from English grammar, not from the eval messages. After the possessive "its" comes a noun or an adjective ("its own", "its main"). After "it's" comes a verb, an article, an adverb or the end of the sentence. "ill" the adjective follows a linking verb or an intensifier ("feel ill", "very ill") or comes before a few fixed nouns ("ill effects").
