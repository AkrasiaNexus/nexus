donna//os ●  local-first planner · macOS 14+

Every planner does what you tell it. Donna doesn't.

She books your goals into the time you actually have, refuses when a booking would cost you a deadline, and spends the night looking for what you should be applying to. Her thinking runs on a model on your own Mac.

⌥D from anywhere

The problem

You already know what matters. The week fills up anyway.

The project, the application, the deadline three weeks out. A calendar holds whatever you put in it, so it fills with whatever is loudest, and the thing that would actually change something slips a day at a time. Auto-schedulers will fit tasks into gaps. None of them will tell you no.

What she does

She acts on her own, and she says what she thinks.

Books what you mean

Say it the way you would to a friend. Slang included: she was taught how you write.

youlowk wanna learn rust 30 mins a day for 2 wks
donnaadded learn rust. 14 sessions, first mon 19:00.

Refuses, and names the cost

Two guards live in code, so the model can't talk its way past them: nothing goes over a deadline that's due, and nothing replaces a session without saying what it costs.

donnathat's your rust session. 4 left and 4 days to do them in — drop one and it doesn't get finished. still?

Notices the pattern

She keeps score of what you book and what you skip, and says it out loud before it becomes a habit.

donna3 saturdays booked, 3 missed. want me to stop booking saturdays?

Searches while you sleep

After ten at night she looks for competitions, programmes and deadlines tied to what you're building, and leads the morning brief with them. Deadlines are read out of the page in code; she never invents one.

brieffound 3 overnight: hackathon (closes dec 04), summer research programme (closes jan 15) and open-source grant.

How it runs

Anything that can be worked out is computed. The model gets the rest.

A 12B model on a laptop is slow and can make things up, so it only handles what is genuinely ambiguous. Everything with a right answer is a pure function: instant, tested, and impossible to hallucinate.

computed · instant · exact
  • The daily brief: your free time, what's due, what's next
  • Free slots and clashes, against your sessions, school and your real calendar feed
  • Dates and reminders: "tomorrow at 5" is 17:00, said back on the real clock
  • Booking confirmations, so a booking costs one model call instead of two
local model · on your mac
  • Understanding what you meant when it could mean two things
  • Choosing the tool: book once, book weekly, add a goal, remember a fact
  • Runs through Ollama. No subscription, no cloud model
  • The overnight search uses a web search API; your plan and messages stay on the machine

Receipts

Tested like it has to work tomorrow morning.

<3show late a reminder may fire in tests, down from up to a minute
  • Reminders never fired in the background. macOS suspends a hidden webview's timers after about five minutes, and Donna is hidden nearly always. Turned off per window, and pinned by a test.
  • "Submit UCAS by jan 15" was booked for next week. The old date reader couldn't express an absolute date. It now reads real dates and times.
  • A calendar event ending in the year 9999 used a gigabyte of memory, and the crash made its hours read as free. Found by an independent security review; feeds are now clamped to the fortnight.
  • Event titles could reach the model's tool channel, next to a tool that clears a day. The model is now only ever told "a calendar commitment".
