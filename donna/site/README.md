# Donna — site and film

`index.html` is the product page. `film.html` is the explainer film, drawn frame by frame and
rendered to `donna.mp4` by `build-film.mjs`; `narration.json` is its script.

## What was used, and why

Asked for: free, open-source tools in the space of [Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai) (AI video
generation with cinematic camera motion) to make the film. Researched November 2026.

### Open-source video generation — the best options, and why the film doesn't use them

| Model | Licence | Strength | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wan 2.2** (Alibaba) | Apache 2.0 | Most versatile; named the best open-source Higgsfield alternative in Crun's comparison | 8 GB VRAM for the 5B model, more for 14B |
| **LTX-2.3** (Lightricks) | open weights | Fastest; generates audio with the video | A GPU |
| **HunyuanVideo 1.5** (Tencent) | open weights | Best motion quality | ~14 GB VRAM with FP8 and offloading |
| Open-Generative-AI | MIT | A self-hosted studio over 200+ models, not a model itself | Whatever the chosen model needs |

All of them need a GPU. The machine this was built on has none (4 CPU cores, 15 GB RAM), and 15 GB
cannot hold even Wan 2.2 5B together with its text encoder. They are also the wrong tool for an
explainer: diffusion video is weak at exact on-screen text and interface, which is most of what
this film shows.

**To add AI-generated shots later**, Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B through ComfyUI is the one to try first: it is
the smallest strong model and the most widely run. It needs a machine with a GPU; a 16 GB Mac would
be memory-bound and slow.

### What the film is made with instead

- **Motion graphics drawn in code.** Every frame of `film.html` is a pure function of time,
  rendered by headless Chromium (Playwright) and encoded with ffmpeg. Deterministic, so the picture
  stays locked to the voice.
- **Voice: Kokoro 82M** (hexgrad, Apache 2.0), voice `af_heart`, through `kokoro-onnx`. The most
  downloaded open text-to-speech model (10.6M downloads a month on Hugging Face) and it runs on a
  CPU — 2.65 s of speech took 0.88 s here. Phonon 31M (Gradium, October 2026) scores slightly higher
  in blind tests but has a much thinner toolchain.
- **Sound bed**: generated by ffmpeg's own tone and noise sources, so there is no music to license.

## Rebuilding the film

```bash
python3 -m venv tts-venv && tts-venv/bin/pip install kokoro-onnx soundfile
```

Download `kokoro-v1.0.onnx` and `voices-v1.0.bin` from the
[kokoro-onnx releases](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx/releases/tag/model-files-v1.0)
into a folder, then from `donna/`:

```bash
tts-venv/bin/python site/speak.py <model-folder> <voice-folder>
```

```bash
node site/build-film.mjs <voice-folder> <output-folder>
```

It scales the gaps between lines (never the speech) so the film lands on exactly sixty seconds,
renders 1920×1080 at 30 fps, and writes `donna.mp4`. `AUDIO_ONLY=1` builds just the sound, which is
the quick way to check a mix.

Every beat is timed once, in `cuesFor()` inside `film.html`, by where its word falls in the spoken
line; the build reads the same cues to place the sound, so a boom cannot drift off its frame. The
sound is all generated by ffmpeg: a synth kick bed that ducks under the voice, booms on the turn,
the refusal and the name, a punch on each cross, glitch ticks on every cut, risers, and a ping when
the reminder lands. Loudness is normalised for the web.

The page hosts files up to 15 MB, so it gets a two-pass encode at 1.75 Mbps:

```bash
ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i <out>/frames/%05d.jpg -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 1750k -pass 1 -an -f mp4 /dev/null
```

```bash
ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i <out>/frames/%05d.jpg -i <out>/mix.wav -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 1750k -pass 2 -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart -c:a aac -b:a 160k -t 60 donna.mp4
```

### Pronunciation

The voice cannot be listened to in the build, so every line's phonemes are read instead:
`k.tokenizer.phonemize(text, "en-us")` in `kokoro-onnx`. The first cut said "Donna lives in…" as
`lˈaɪvz` (as in *live music*); words that read two ways are kept out of the script.
