# Concept & story: Creative Director + Story Architect

## 1. Find the thesis (one sentence)
Write the one sentence the site must prove, e.g. "Small daily actions add up to measurable change."
Then ask whether the way the page moves supports that sentence or contradicts it (Why Zero gates its scenes because its argument is "learning = doing").
If you can't say it in one sentence, the jury won't feel it.

## 2. Pick the signature (choose ONE, name two rejected options)
Award winners own one thing completely:
- **Object.** One hero thing driven by scroll, the rest editorial and fast. Lando Norris → the helmet; EcoSphere → the phone. Best default for products and apps.
- **Material.** One substance rendered better than anyone: Igloo → ice; Infinite Passerella → cloth. Good for brands with a tactile identity.
- **Verb.** Give the visitor something to *do*: deliver (Messenger), hold-to-advance (Santioni), drag-to-explore (Unseen). Good when agency is the point.
- **Place.** The scroll is a walk through one location (Persepolis Reimagined, Codrops moon story). Good for venues, heritage, factories, events.
- **Type.** Kinetic typography as the main event (Codrops "Do not scroll"). Good for manifestos, agencies, launches with no product shot.

Concept generators to try before settling:
- **Literalise the product's core action.** A scanner app → the phone physically turns to scan; a budgeting app → coins fall into jars as you scroll.
- **Before/after transformation.** The signature changes state across the story (dark → lit, empty → full, seed → tree).
- **Zoom ladder.** Macro → micro → macro: the object, inside the object, the world around it.
- **Metaphor mining.** List 10 nouns from the product's world, keep the one with the best motion verb.
- **Constraint pick.** "Only one colour plus ink" or "only type, no images" often beats more stuff.

## 2b. Two studio methods that keep winning
- **Make the concept behave.** Name the idea in two words, then make the signature *act it out*. Dash Creative: "magnetic commerce", so the 3D logo pulls toward the cursor. EcoSphere: "scan to sort", so the phone turns and scans. If the motion could belong to any other brand, it isn't the concept.
- **One brand asset persists as the interface.** Obys starts from its typeface; the logo transforms old to new and stays on screen through every layout, becoming UI. Pick one asset (logo, product, mascot, word) and let it travel through the whole scroll.
- **Restraint is the hard part.** Podium chose one continuous sequence over pages and said the main challenge was "not what to build, but what to remove". Cut a chapter before you polish a weak one.
- **Decide touch behaviour at concept time**, not after. Dash: "the experience changes significantly on touch devices; consider it earlier".

When the user gives no concept, generate **3 concepts** with [concept-bank.md](concept-bank.md) as a springboard. Pitch each in one line (thesis · signature · signature moment), pick one with a reason, and note the two rejected in docs/STORY.md.

## 3. Arc and pacing
- Default **5 scenes** (hook 0–15%, context 15–35%, journey 35–70%, climax 70–85%, resolution 85–100%). Visitors give 2–3 minutes.
- More chapters are fine **only** if each is short and transitions are continuous (EcoSphere has 10 at ~110vh each). A **loop** structure (end returns to start) may exceed 5.
- The **hook must land in the first second**: something already alive on load (logo light, object reacting to the cursor). A plain fade is a weak hook.
- Put the **signature moment at the climax** (70–85%), not at the top.
- End on a **clear path forward**: CTA buttons, then a minimal footer.

- Note what carries over between chapters (continuity is the craft).
- 3+ genuine interaction points across the site (scroll scrub, cursor light, drag, hover reveals, CTA).

## 5. Copy voice
Short, concrete, confident. Highlight 1–2 key words per headline (colour/weight, revealed by the timeline).
Demo data says "demo"/"sample" on the element itself. Real product names, real features. No lorem ipsum.
